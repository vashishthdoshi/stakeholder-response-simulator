Stakeholder Reaction Simulator

Pre-test crisis statements against a simulated stakeholder panel before they go out.

What this tool does

In a crisis, a statement is judged by several audiences at once, and each reads it differently. This tool runs a draft statement past a simulated panel of employees, customers, journalists, investors and regulators. Each stakeholder reacts in their own voice, points to the exact phrases that trigger them, and says what would change their mind.

Alongside the panel, rule checks flag numbers that do not match confirmed facts, mentions of facts that are still unconfirmed or withdrawn, and phrases your team has agreed to avoid. Each persona runs more than once, and where its reactions disagree the result is marked uncertain.

It is a pre-mortem aid for the drafting and approval loop. It does not replace real stakeholder research, legal review or human sign-off.

How it works

  1. Set the scene on this tab. Public context, a fact sheet with the status of each fact, phrases to avoid, and one or two drafts.
  2. Run the panel on the next tab. Choose and edit personas, then run the simulation.
  3. Read and compare. Sentiment and trust by stakeholder, flagged phrases in the text, rule-check findings, and a side-by-side comparison of the two drafts. Results export as Markdown or JSON.

The sample scenario

Fictional, preloaded so the tool can be tried immediately.

The company
Northwind Logistics, a national parcel delivery company.
What happened
Unauthorized access to the shipment-tracking database was detected on October 9 and closed on October 10. News surfaced on social media on October 11, before any company statement, and a reporter has posted an unconfirmed figure of 50,000 customers.
What is confirmed
About 48,000 customers had names, email addresses and shipping addresses exposed. A forensic firm is engaged and regulators were notified.
What is still open
Whether payment data was involved. An early claim that only business accounts were affected has been withdrawn.
The two drafts
Draft A is vague and defensive, with an unsupported figure and stock phrases. Draft B is specific, apologizes and commits to a date for the next update.

Test your own scenario

Everything below is editable. To test a different situation, change these fields, then continue to the stakeholder test:

  1. Public context. Describe only what stakeholders can already see: the news, social posts, reported figures. Keep internal information out, since the personas react as outsiders.
  2. Fact sheet. One fact per line, in the form ID | confirmed, unconfirmed or withdrawn | fact | watch terms. Numbers in confirmed facts are what drafts get checked against. Add watch terms to unconfirmed or withdrawn facts, so any draft that touches them is flagged.
  3. Phrases to avoid. Your team's list, one per line.
  4. Drafts. Paste one statement, or two to compare.
  5. Personas. On the next tab, rename and rewrite the personas to match your real audiences, and switch off any you do not need.

Use fictional or approved, non-confidential material only. Sample results exist only for the preloaded drafts, so once you edit them, run live.
