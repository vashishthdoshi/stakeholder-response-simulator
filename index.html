Stakeholder Reaction Simulator

Pre-test crisis communication against a simulated stakeholder panel before it goes out.

What this tool does

In a crisis, one statement is judged by several audiences at once, and each reads it differently. This tool runs your draft statements past a simulated panel of stakeholders, such as employees, customers, journalists, investors and regulators. Each one reacts in their own voice, points to the phrases that trigger them, and says what would most improve their reaction.

Scoring: Every stakeholder starts at -5, the worst takeaway at the start of the crisis. A statement earns up to 2 points each for clarity, transparency, positivity, communal outlook and apologetic framing, so scores run from -5 to +5. A score of 0 means the stakeholder feels neither strongly negative nor positive.

Head to head: With two drafts, each stakeholder also compares them side by side, in random order, and says which better addresses their concerns.

Fact check: Each draft is checked against your fact sheet for factual problems only: figures that contradict confirmed facts, open questions stated as settled, and withdrawn claims repeated. Rounded or approximate figures are noted, not failed. Tone and confident phrasing are left alone unless you list them as phrases to avoid.

How it works

  1. Set the scene and the panel on this tab: Public context, fact sheet, phrases to avoid, one or two drafts, and the personas.
  2. Run the simulation on the next tab: Personas run more than once, so you can see how stable each reaction is.
  3. Read and compare: Scores by stakeholder, the head-to-head verdict, fact-check findings, a ranked list of fixes, and each stakeholder's reaction. Results export as Markdown or JSON.

The sample scenario

Fictional, preloaded so the tool can be tried immediately.

The company
Northwind Logistics, a national parcel delivery company.
What happened
Unauthorized access to the shipment-tracking database was detected on October 9 and closed on October 10. News surfaced on social media on October 11, before any company statement, and a reporter has posted an unconfirmed figure of 50,000 customers.
What is confirmed
About 48,000 customers had names, email addresses and shipping addresses exposed. A forensic firm is engaged and regulators were notified.
Open questions
Whether payment data was involved. An early claim that only business accounts were affected has been withdrawn.
The two drafts
Draft A is vague and defensive, with an unsupported figure and stock phrases. Draft B is specific, apologizes and commits to a date for the next update.

Test your own scenario

Everything below is editable. To test a different situation, change these fields, then continue to the next tab:

  1. Public context: Only what stakeholders can already see: the news, social posts, reported figures. Personas react as outsiders and never see the fact sheet.
  2. Fact sheet: Facts, in the format - ID | confirmed, unconfirmed or withdrawn | fact. It is used only by the fact check.
  3. Phrases to avoid: Optional. Your team's list, one per line. Leave it empty if your team is comfortable with stock phrases.
  4. Drafts: Paste one statement, or two to compare.
  5. Personas: Rewrite them to match the audiences of your crisis, and switch off any you do not need. Personas written for one crisis give misleading results on another.

Use fictional or approved, non-confidential material only. Sample results exist only for the preloaded scenario, so once you edit it, run live.
